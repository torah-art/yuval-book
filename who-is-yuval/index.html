Who Is Yuval?

By Ephraim Kalish

“And there shall be upon every high mountain, and upon every high hill, rivers and streams of waters.” Isaiah 30:25, KJV

Yuval (Jubal) is the first person whose art the Bible singles out in a special way. He was the father, that is, the teacher and founder, of the art of music.

In his commentary on Lev 2:14, Ibn Ezra explains: «אָבִיב נקרא כן בעבור שהוא ראשון והוא מגזרת אָב» — «aviv is so called because it is the first, and [the word] is from the root av». ‹EV› — «the first shoot», and ‹AVIV› — «the first ears of barley». In a broader sense ‹AV› is the source of something, the originating beginning.

Here the Bible speaks of the birth of culture and art as we understand them today. In connection with Yuval and his brothers, Scripture mentions for the first time musical instruments, crafts and arts that have come down to us and remain relevant to this day. Their creative work is all the more interesting because the Bible calls them fathers.

It is remarkable that Lion Feuchtwanger, in his novel «Josephus», tells of a certain Levite. This Levite was one of the few who survived the fall of Judea and of the city of the great king. He served as a singer in the Jerusalem Temple until its destruction by the Romans. The writer gives the surviving servant an unusual, symbolic name: Yuval ben Yuval. In effect the name means «Musician, son of a Musician». It is no accident that the writer gives exactly this name to the keeper of the Temple’s musical tradition: he points to a thousand-year tradition and to the succession from the first musician on earth.

Again and again I use the words «led by God» of Yuval. That is his name in translation: «led» (one who follows someone). It is formed from ‹YOVÉL› (carrying, leading). It can be translated as «leader, chief».

The names of all three brothers come from one root. Even an unmusical ear will catch it: Yaval — Yuval — Tuval. The middle one’s name literally repeats a word from the 53rd chapter of the prophet Isaiah: «As a sheep he was led to the slaughter». We are especially interested in the word «led», ‹YUVÁL›: the one who is led, and who is like a sheep, that is, obediently follows the shepherd.

Let us look at other words with the same root; they matter for understanding the names and callings of the brothers. In ancient Hebrew all these words are written the same way as the name Yuval and differ only in their vowel points:

YUVÁL — a spring; a stream of water breaking through the earth’s crust. From captivity the stream bursts out into freedom and gives rise to a full-flowing river. And as the current of water is directed by its banks, so the words of God are the banks for Yuval’s creative work. Yuval is led by the Most High.

YEVÚL (harvest) — another possible way to interpret the name of Yuval and his brothers.

YOVÉL — a ram. The Talmud explains the essence of this word thus: the ram walks at the head of the flock and leads it in the right direction, being itself led by the shepherd and obedient to him. So Yuval and his brothers are obedient to the voice of the Most High and led many after them.

YOVÉL — a ram’s horn. This horn is blown on special, important occasions. It heralds great times. So Yuval’s creative work became the herald of turning-point events. Seven YOVÉL horns played a decisive role in the destruction of the walls of Jericho, when God brought Israel out of Egyptian bondage and into the land promised to the fathers. The SHOFÁR (another name for the ram’s horn) plays a special role at the giving of the Torah to Israel on Mount Sinai.

YOVÉL — the jubilee year. This is the year in which the special ram’s horn, the YOVÉL, is blown. That is precisely why such a year is called ‹YOVÉL› (jubilee). The essence of the commandment of the jubilee is expressed in the song of the prophet Isaiah: «The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me; because the LORD hath anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek; he hath sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to them that are bound; to proclaim the acceptable year of the LORD» [Isa 61:1–2]. «The acceptable year of the LORD» is the jubilee, ‹YOVÉL›. The prophet turns in poetic form to the law of Moshe about the jubilee. The meaning of the jubilee is complete liberation, the restoration of what was lost. And here it is fitting to remember that the descendants of Cain, the three brothers, are the eighth generation from Adam. In Jewish tradition the number eight is a symbol of a new beginning, and a new beginning is the essence of the jubilee. This is the deep meaning of the words of the Most High about the seven generations from Cain (eight from Adam).

In the phrase «the father of all who play the harp and flute», the word ‹TOFÉS› («who play») would be more accurately translated from ancient Hebrew as «the father of everyone who masters the kinor and the ugav». Translated literally, the verb ‹TOFÉS› means «to seize, to take possession of, to appropriate [territory], to hold firmly». When we speak of musical instruments, this means a seizing and mastering of them such that they become the territory, a part, of the one who plays them. The instrument seems to merge with the musician: the violin becomes an extension of the soul, the strings of the heart and the voice of the violinist.

There is another word related to ‹TOFÉS› (to seize): ‹TIPÉS› (to climb, to ascend). This word gives rise to a further understanding: mastering musical instruments requires an ascent from the musician, when all his strength, physical and spiritual, is strained as in climbing a mountain. To reach perfection in his skill on a musical instrument, he climbs ever higher up invisible crags, gripping ledges and setting his feet on steps not made by hands. Creative work is always an ascent.

Was Yuval the inventor and maker of musical instruments? This we cannot assert. Apparently, he improved them. Perhaps he made the number of the kinor’s strings equal to seven, the expression of fullness, like the seven days of the week, the seven colours and the seven notes. It is possible that it was he who put a reed into the flute of his father Lemekh, thus likening it to the human vocal cords and its sound to the most perfect musical instrument of the son of Adam. Some researchers claim that Yuval’s ugav is an ancient ancestor of the modern Armenian duduk.

A medieval midrash says that musical instruments were created by angels but were broken after the fall of the heavenly beings who rebelled against the Creator, which greatly grieved God’s messengers. Then Yuval gathered the instruments piece by piece and recreated them in their original form.

What we can say with certainty, on the basis of the text of the Torah, is that he became the father of everyone who masters musical instruments. That is, something deep was revealed to him, and he was able to pass his revelation on to his disciples.

Yuval’s name can be interpreted thus: a breakthrough, a striving for the new, a constant search. Yuval discovered music as a language of communion with the Creator, as a way of turning to God and of worshipping the Most High. It would be more correct to say that God placed this revelation in him.

Of Yuval it is said: «The father of all who play the harp and flute». In the language of the Torah these instruments are called kinor (harp) and ugav (flute). They are meant to reveal something of Yuval’s insight, which he passed on to us. They carry an encoded message. In Hebrew the names of his musical instruments contain his teaching in concentrated form, just as a seed contains the future plant.
